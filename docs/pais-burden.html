PAIS burden and ME

Notes:pais/README.md

An illustrative model of post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) in the Netherlands. With the default assumptions, ME is about half of PAIS patients (0.52% of 1.06% of the population) but about 80% of the PAIS disease burden, because ME is much more disabling than most PAIS without ME. A second view sketches how Long COVID, ME, dysautonomia and MCAS may overlap. Every input can be changed on the topic page. Note: these numbers are not grounded in hard data. They're my guesses of absolute and relative prevalence in The Netherlands based on reading the literature.

How Long COVID, ME, dysautonomia and MCAS may overlap

Assumptions: dysautonomia and MCAS

  • Dysautonomia in ME. At a Dutch clinic, 25% of 627 adults with ME and orthostatic symptoms met POTS criteria on tilt testing (van Campen 2018). Broader orthostatic intolerance is more common, so 50% is an assumption for OI and POTS combined.
  • Dysautonomia in Long COVID. 31% of 467 Swedish people with Long COVID on at least half-time sick leave had POTS (Karolinska 2025). That is a severely affected group, part of which has ME. Small studies report 19% to 63%.
  • MCAS. There are no prevalence figures based on diagnostic criteria. There is only an online symptom survey of 136 people recruited from Facebook groups (Weinstock 2021). The MCAS shares are guesses.
  • Disease burden. Everyone gets the severity from the burden model (ME, or Long COVID without ME). Dysautonomia and MCAS are added on top, combined the way the GBD does it: 1 − (1−a)(1−b)(1−c). The extra severity is an assumption. For comparison, POTS patients score 0.56 on the EQ-5D against 0.87 in the general population, but that includes their ME and Long COVID (AAN 2025).

  • ME, not housebound: 0.22. The GBD weight for "infectious disease, post-acute consequences" (fatigue, pain, low mood) is 0.219. An Australian study found an EQ-5D of 0.69 in mild ME against 0.89 in the population, a loss of 0.20 (abstract, later withdrawn).
  • ME, house/bedbound: 0.55. In the same study, moderate ME (ICC: mostly housebound) gave a loss of 0.45; severe ME scored much lower. For comparison: GBD moderate MS 0.46, severe MS 0.72. Mean ME utility in patient associations is 0.46–0.47 (DK, AU), but those groups are sicker than average.
  • Long COVID without ME: 0.07 (other) and 0.40 (housebound). GBD weights for Long COVID: mild cognitive symptoms 0.069, mild respiratory symptoms 0.019, severe 0.38–0.41 (JAMA 2022). In a Japanese population study, Long COVID scored 0.94 against 0.97 (INQUIRY 2024).
  • Post-vax (0.0037%) is an assumption, with the same split as Long COVID. Post-Legionella (0.0083%): 300 new cases a year in the Netherlands (the Dutch PAIS Alliance cites 500) × an average illness duration of about 5 years, as for post-Lyme = 1,500 people.
  • Post-Lyme, post-Q fever, post-Legionella and post-sepsis without ME: 0.07. The same assumption as mild Long COVID. ME after these triggers gets the average ME severity. The share of ME after these triggers has never been measured.
  • Prevalence is the share of the population, converted from Dutch estimates (population 18.0 million). Burden is DALYs per person per year: prevalence × severity, so 1% means the population loses 1% of its healthy life-years. Starting point: the Dutch PAIS Alliance, whose figures are divided by 3 here (post-Q fever unchanged; post-Legionella as above). Post-Lyme is my own estimate (LymeProspect, RIVM).
