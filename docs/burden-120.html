Research vs disease burden 120 diseases

Notes:expanded/README.md

Papers and trials per year (2021–2025) set against global DALYs (GBD 2023) for 120 diseases. ME/CFS has 10.3× fewer papers (90% range 7.5–14.5) and 7.7× fewer trials (5.0–11.7) than the trend predicts for its burden. On papers, only neonatal encephalopathy falls further below. The trend is loose (R² 0.39 for papers, 0.38 for trials).

Caveats
  • The trend is rough: R² 0.39 (papers) and 0.38 (trials). A typical disease sits a factor of 3 (papers) or 4 (trials) off the line.
  • Burden is global. Diseases mainly in low-income regions mostly fall below the trend (fewer papers and trials than their burden predicts), partly because their burden falls where little research is done. Cancers mostly sit above it.
  • “Mainly low-income regions” (teal) are diseases much less common in high-income countries: all neglected tropical diseases, plus malaria, tuberculosis, typhoid, measles, rabies, dengue, yellow fever, meningitis, pertussis, hepatitis B, rheumatic heart disease, neonatal sepsis, neonatal encephalopathy, maternal haemorrhage, sickle cell disease and thalassaemia. They stay in the trend fit; the table above shows the result without them.
  • Comparator burden is from GBD 2023 (95% interval). ME/CFS and long COVID burden is from the ME/CFS Burden model v11.2, CCC criteria, year 2026 (90% range). Groups are not used to fit the trend.
  • Papers titled only “long COVID” are not in the ME/CFS count, so the ME/CFS shortfall is slightly overstated.
  • Shortfall ranges combine burden uncertainty (lognormal fitted to each burden interval) and trend-fit uncertainty (4,000 bootstrap resamples of the 120 comparators). Random seed 2558632273.
  • The linear view shows the same trend (fitted on log values), so it curves. On linear axes a few diseases with very large burden stretch the chart, and most points crowd near zero. The zoom buttons (4×, 8×) show the bottom left of the linear view; dots outside the frame are left out.
  • Trends are refitted here from the rounded counts in the CSV. They match the file's trend columns exactly for papers and within 2% for trials.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov only.

Trials in all registries (WHO ICTRP)

Notes:expanded/ictrp/README.md

ClinicalTrials.gov misses trials registered elsewhere. All registries hold 2.5× as many ME/CFS trials (1.8× without the two Chinese registries), against a median of 1.9× for 18 comparison diseases. Adjusted for this, the ME/CFS trial shortfall is about 6–8×. 29% of ME/CFS trials are not mainly in ME/CFS patients, between MS (17%) and RA (38%).
