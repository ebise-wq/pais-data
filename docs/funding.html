Public biomedical R&D funding and output 2024

Notes:funding/README.md

Government biomedical R&D budgets set against medical papers and citations, 19 countries. The US spends 32 bn PPS; China's budget is a guess (about 14 bn). Papers grow more slowly than budgets (log-log slope 0.76). Portugal publishes most per unit of budget, Japan and the US least. Seven budgets are estimates, so this is a rough signal, not an efficiency ranking.

Notes and caveats
  • Budgets are government budget allocations for R&D (Eurostat GBARD, 2024), the sum of three objectives: directed health funding (NABS07), the medical share of university block funds (NABS123) and other medical R&D (NABS133). PPS (purchasing power standards) convert currencies so one unit buys the same basket of goods everywhere.
  • Estimated (hatched): Japan, UK, South Korea, Italy, Canada, Australia, Norway. Built from national budgets or scaled from partial reporting, so treat their exact position as indicative. Tap a bar or dot for how each was built.
  • China publishes no comparable figure. The 14 bn PPS shown assumes health is 6–12% of government R&D (range 9.4–18.7), so its ratios could be half or double. Its 66% self-citation rate is the highest here.
  • EU institutions are a funder, not a country: about €2.5 bn a year from the EU budget, on top of the national figures, adding roughly 15%.
  • United States reports only directed funding, but that is comprehensive: NIH grants include the overhead that Europe pays from university block funds. Same money, different column. Flag: provisional.
  • United Kingdom left Eurostat after Brexit; built from NIHR, MRC and the medical share of Research England block grants. Canada is federal only.
  • Turkey is reported, but about 95% is university block funds apportioned by Eurostat, first reported in 2024. Other flags: Netherlands series break; Japan and Austria defined differently.
  • Output is documents and citations in the Medicine subject area of SCImago (Scopus), 2024. Citations exclude self-citations and had about a year to accrue. A co-authored paper counts once for each country. Ireland is left out of the output views.
  • Not an efficiency ranking. The fit slope below 1 is partly artefact: error in the budget axis pulls it down, and rich systems pay more per unit of research than a whole-economy price index implies.
