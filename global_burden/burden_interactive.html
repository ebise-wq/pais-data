How the estimate is built

Nobody has counted people with ME/CFS worldwide. So the model starts from the best studies in a few countries and corrects them step by step. Each correction is a range, not a single number. The computer draws from all the ranges 300,000 times, which gives the spread you see in the charts.

The ME/CFS total has two parts. Classic ME started before or without COVID. Post-COVID ME is estimated twice, from different studies: once from a US study of infected people and once from a UK survey of long COVID. The two routes count equally, and the page uses their average. Then people are turned into disease burden. A separate route estimates all long COVID. The same model gives figures for 21 rich countries.

Checks against other evidence

The 2040 projection

A projection, not a forecast. It assumes COVID keeps circulating and today's patterns hold, and it allows for another pandemic. The inputs about new infections are judgement.

National figures

For 21 rich countries the model uses the same rates as for rich countries in general, and changes three things: the number of people in each age group (UN World Population Prospects 2024), the share of first infections that came before Omicron, and the share of people vaccinated when Omicron arrived. Countries that kept COVID out until Omicron, like Australia and New Zealand, get fewer post-COVID cases per person.

The share infected before Omicron comes from confirmed cases up to mid-December 2021 (Our World in Data), multiplied by IHME's estimate of infections per confirmed case where it gives one for that country (Barber 2022), and by 1.9–3.0 otherwise. Vaccination is the share fully vaccinated in mid-January 2022 (Our World in Data); for Switzerland we use 66%. Long COVID starts from the UK survey, adjusted for each country's ages and its infection history compared with the UK. Not included: real differences in classic ME/CFS between countries, which nobody has measured with the same method, and differences in how many reinfections people have had since 2022.

From people to disease burden

Disease burden is counted in disability-adjusted life years (DALYs): healthy years lost in one year, to illness and to early death. For each group the model multiplies three things: the number of people living with it, the health each loses in a year (the disability weight), and an addition for early death.

How severe ME/CFS is

The International Consensus Criteria describe four levels. The bar shows the mix we assume for the criteria you picked.

Two large patient surveys give the best picture. In DecodeME (UK, 17,074 people with a GP diagnosis) 34% rated themselves mild, 53% moderate, 12% severe and 0.7% very severe. In the European ME Alliance survey (11,109 people) it was 24%, 54%, 16% and 2.4%. Both miss people: the very severe can rarely fill in a survey, so we raise that share to 3–6%. Most people who meet the criteria have no diagnosis and tend to be milder, so we also raise the mild share. Stricter criteria select sicker people.

How much health is lost

A disability weight says how much health a condition takes away, from 0 (none) to 1 (as bad as death). A year lived with a weight of 0.4 counts as losing 0.4 healthy years. The Global Burden of Disease study sets its weights by asking thousands of people to compare short descriptions of health states. Nobody has done this for ME/CFS. So we matched each severity level to states with similar loss of function that do have a weight.

From rich countries to the whole world

Almost all good studies come from rich countries. None of these effects has been measured in a low- or middle-income country.

Why these sources

For each input we looked for the study least likely to be biased, and passed over figures that are often quoted but weakly founded.

Chicago community study (Jason 1999)
classic ME/CFS
The researchers phoned a random sample of 28,673 households, then had doctors examine everyone who screened positive. Very few studies do both. It found 0.42% of adults. We passed over the often-quoted 0.89% from a 2020 review (Lim 2020), because it pools questionnaire surveys with doctors' diagnoses and its studies disagree tenfold. We also passed over a 2007 Georgia study whose broad definition counted many people with depression.
English primary care (Nacul 2011) and Dutch patients (MECVS Nederland 2023)
converting definitions
Both checked the same patients against several definitions, so the ratios between definitions compare like with like. The Dutch patients were members of a patient organisation, who tend to be sicker than average, so we shifted those ratios down.
RECOVER study, United States (Vernon 2025)
post-COVID ME
The largest study that also followed people who had not been infected, so it can separate ME caused by COVID from ME that would have happened anyway. We use only the people who joined soon after infection: those who joined when already ill met the criteria four times as often (6.3% against 1.6%). We passed over "half of long COVID patients have ME/CFS", which comes from specialist clinics where the sickest patients end up. We also passed over "400 million people with long COVID", which is a US survey rate multiplied out to the world.
DecodeME and European ME Alliance surveys
severity mix
The two largest samples that report severity, with 17,074 and 11,109 people. Both are surveys, so they miss the very severe, who can rarely respond, and people without a diagnosis, who tend to be milder. We adjust for both by judgement. Older figures of a quarter severe or worse come from a 2000 charity member survey and clinics, which see sicker people (Grach 2023).
German hospital staff (Tack 2026)
check, post-COVID ME
The only study we found where doctors assessed the people with lasting fatigue in a defined group of infected people. 7 of 221 (3.2%) met CCC about 21 months after an early-variant infection, before vaccination. It is small, has no uninfected comparison and is mostly middle-aged women, so we use it to widen the range rather than as the anchor.
US veterans study (Xie 2024)
vaccination
Medical records of 441,583 infected veterans, compared with 4.7 million uninfected, split by variant and vaccination. It is the only source we found that separates the two. Veterans are older and mostly male, and the outcome is long COVID in general, not ME.
Global Burden of Disease long COVID analysis (Wulf Hanson 2022)
long COVID burden
The Global Burden of Disease study's own estimate, from 54 studies and 1.2 million people, adjusted for health before COVID. Its average weight, 0.23, is for people with clear symptom clusters, while a quarter of the UK survey's cases say long COVID does not limit them at all. So we set weights by how limited people are, on the same Global Burden of Disease scale as the reference diseases.
UK Coronavirus Infection Survey (ONS)
long COVID, checks
A large random sample of households (ONS, winter 2023–24). People described their own symptoms, and the question about severity had no uninfected comparison group, so we correct for symptoms not caused by infection. It is the base for the all-long-COVID route and a check on the post-COVID ME route.
Dubbo study, Australia (Hickie 2006)
check only
Infections were confirmed in the lab and patients were followed for a year. About 11% met ME/CFS criteria six months later, whether the trigger was a virus or a bacterium.
Global Burden of Disease 2010 disability weights
disability weight
Weights only compare fairly if they are measured the same way, so we use only Global Burden of Disease weights, matched to ME/CFS by level of function. We left out quality-of-life scores (EQ-5D) from ME/CFS patients. They sit on a different scale, and the only published conversion is a small Belgian pilot (Charafeddine 2017) whose results were not consistent; it found such scores ran about 0.30 above the matching Global Burden of Disease weights.
London health records study (Roberts 2016)
early death
The largest study of deaths among diagnosed patients. It found no excess deaths overall but more suicides, though based on few deaths. We passed over US memorial lists, which only count deaths that someone chose to report.
UN World Population Prospects 2024 and Global Burden of Disease 2021
population, comparisons
The standard sources for population by age and for the burden of other diseases.
